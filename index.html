First principles

Why an index beats federated search

Most AI tools use federated search: they query each app live at the moment of the question. Glean indexes content, people, permissions and activity ahead of time. Select a principle.

The moat

Easy to demo, hard to run at enterprise scale

Build versus buy

Many banks start by building retrieval on Azure OpenAI or Bedrock. The model is the easy part. Connecting data is phase one. Phase two is changing how work gets done: where agents help, how to govern them, how to measure them. Glean Transform maps how work happens and measures impact, and Forrester's composite showed a 141% three-year ROI.

06Deal and measurement

Where these deals stall, how we qualify them, and how we prove value

Six stalls we plan for from the first meeting, the MEDDPICC checklist every opportunity runs against, and the scorecard every pilot is judged on.

"We already bought Copilot"

We coexist first and offer a side-by-side: Copilot alone vs. Copilot with Glean context on the same questions. A disappointing Copilot pilot is good hunting ground.

"Model risk and TPRM will take a year"

Expect the Microsoft read and write permissions question from security. The security, TPRM and model risk package goes out at M2, mapped to NYDFS Part 500, with the SE in the room. The CISO joins at M2, not at signature.

"We'll build it on Azure or Bedrock"

We price the build honestly over three years: connectors, permission sync, ranking, evaluation and run cost. Then we offer Glean's APIs so their engineers build firm-specific agents on top.

The pilot becomes a science project

A pilot is a closing tool, not a way to keep talking. No SE time until pain, a champion and an economic buyer are confirmed. Then one workflow, written success criteria and expansion terms agreed up front. Gray-area deals are time-boxed and escalated.

Production approvals are the long pole

Glean pilots run on production connections, not sandboxes, and every system owner approves separately. At the largest banks, approval alone can take three months. We map each owner at M2 and start approvals before the pilot is scoped.

One champion, one thread

A single champion is a death sentence. The best cycles pair a C-level sponsor with a senior IT or AI leader and two or three line-of-business stakeholders. A deal still single-threaded after six months gets paid resources or we pull back.

MEDDPICC checklist

Updated after every call and reviewed with each AE weekly. Select a letter to flip it to the proof required before a deal counts in the forecast.

Commit ruleA deal enters commit only when the economic buyer has confirmed the metrics, the pilot has met written success criteria, the paper process including TPRM and production connector approvals is dated in a mutual plan, and the champion has been tested.

How we prove value

Measure adoption and outcomes, not queries

Searches run, licenses deployed and tokens consumed are easy to game and prove nothing to a CFO. Every pilot is scored on outcomes the customer already cares about.

What we do not sell on

  • Searches run or licenses deployed
  • Tokens consumed
  • A productivity percentage promised before discovery
  • Cost only

What we measure

  • Weekly active use in the pilot group, against the 45% benchmark across Glean customers
  • Hours saved on named workflows: client prep, DDQs, claims, service tickets
  • Cost per AI task against the tools they already pay for

How it expands

A pilot proves one workflow in one department. That result becomes the champion's case for the next department, seats go firm-wide, and governed agents follow on the same context. Expansion is proven at Glean, so a smaller first land that gets us in the door is worth taking.

Partnering with Solutions Engineering

The AE, the SE and I go into every strategic deal together

SE capacity is the scarcest resource in the region, so I protect it.

Qualify before SE time

An opportunity gets an SE once pain, a champion and an economic buyer are confirmed. AEs carry the first executive conversations themselves. Target one SE for every 1.75 AEs or better.

Forecast together

The RVP and the SE leader each call the number. Where the calls differ, we have found a gap neither of us saw alone, and we close it.

Disagree, then go in together

At Honeycomb, my SE leader flagged feasibility risk on a pilot the team wanted to close that quarter. We extended it, she was right, and the deal expanded. That friction built the partnership.
